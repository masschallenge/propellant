# Photography and video

## What to show

- Every image carries some warmth: skin tones, natural or lamp light, wood, brick, city lights.
- Real work and real people: labs, workbenches, prototypes, conversations. Founders, mentors,
  partners and staff doing something together.
- Mostly candid moments, with the occasional founder hero portrait.
- The whole community: a range of genders, ages, backgrounds and abilities.
- Asymmetric frames that leave room for text, and a range that includes wide shots.

## How it is shot

- Natural light and balanced exposure, with soft flash for dark rooms.
- Shot in RAW and edited only for exposure and contrast.
- No artistic filters and no black and white.

## Avoid

- People on phones or looking bored, visible alcohol, crowds from behind and cluttered
  compositions.
- Black and white.
- Generic stock: handshakes, posed smiles, abstract tech with no people.
- Obviously AI generated images: waxy skin, warped hands or text, impossible lighting, glowing
  suns, lens flares, holograms.
- The full-frame cool duotone, which is retired because it reads clinical.
- Overlays so heavy the subject disappears.

## Sources

- MassChallenge's own professional event photography comes first. Its photos and videos are in
  the brand guide's photo library (propellant.masschallenge.org/brand/#photos). Some of those
  photos carry the retired teal duotone: use them as they are, and never duotone a new photo.
- Licensed stock photography that MassChallenge holds a license for may be used when it looks
  believable and follows these rules. Ask MassChallenge Marketing for it.
- Location photos for each site, by city in the US and by country elsewhere, follow the same rules.
- Use photos the person provides only if they have the rights. Never pull images from a web search
  or social media.
- Never present a stock or AI generated image as a real founder, partner, startup or event. Never
  generate images of real, named people.
- Only feature identifiable people prominently, or name them, if they agreed.

## Treatment

| Treatment        | Use it for                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01 Overlay       | Heroes, social posts and event graphics. A thin hero overlay: a linear gradient from Navy-black `#051925` on the text side to Ocean `#037AA6` toward the subject, at 15 to 30% opacity, so warm light glows through. |
| 02 Full color    | Content photos, spaces and people. No overlay and no duotone.                                                                                                                                                        |
| 03 Accent rescue | A cool photo with no warmth gets one warm accent, Yellow, Orange or Lime, on a tag, a rule or a subhead.                                                                                                             |

- Judge by eye: if warm areas go gray or blue, the overlay is too heavy.
- Text goes only on a calm, high contrast area of a photo. Otherwise move it, add the hero overlay,
  or set it on a solid Navy, Black or Deep Teal block.

In HTML, the overlay is a second background layer over the photo, for example
`background: linear-gradient(90deg, rgb(5 25 37 / 0.3), rgb(3 122 166 / 0.3)), url(photo.jpg)`
with the text on the left. Turn the angle so the dark end sits under the words.

## Cropping and size

- Crop on purpose: keep faces and heads whole, never crop at the neck, elbows or knees, and leave
  lead room in the direction people face.
- Never stretch or squash. Keep proportions.
- One strong photo beats a collage.
- On websites and in graphics, photos have square corners. Photos people upload, such as profile
  photos, are shown whole and never auto cropped.

## Video

- The photography rules apply: real people, balanced exposure and warmth in the frame.
- Every video uses the standard logo open and end slates and consistent lower thirds: a Navy block
  with a Cyan rule, the name in Oswald SemiBold and the title in Lato, bottom left, inside the
  title safe area.
- Frame interviews on the rule of thirds, with the eyes near a top intersection and lead room in
  the direction the subject faces.
- Only use footage MassChallenge has licensed or shot itself, and licensed music.
- Final exports are approved by MassChallenge Marketing before they go live.
- Captions on every video. Play on click, never autoplay with sound.

## Alt text

One sentence on what matters in the image, such as "Founders pitching to judges at demo day". Name
people only if they agreed to be featured. Mark decorative images as decorative.

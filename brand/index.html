Graphic devices

The shapes, frames and treatments that make MassChallenge work recognizable. Build them from the palette and the logo files.

Geometric pattern

MC Blue triangles with a scatter of light tint triangles, and an Oswald label in capitals on top, such as ACCELERATOR or CHALLENGE.

Use it for: Covers and headers for a program or a page.

Set the label in Black (5.7:1 on MC Blue, 15.1:1 on the light tint), or in White on a solid Navy or Deep Teal panel (15.3:1 and 8.9:1). Never set it in White straight on the triangles: White on MC Blue is only 2.8:1, below the 3:1 that even large text needs.

Hero overlay

A thin linear gradient over a photo, from Navy-black on the text side to Ocean toward the subject, at 15 to 30% opacity. It replaces the retired teal duotone.

Use it for: Heroes, social posts and event graphics.

Keep it light enough that warm light glows through. If warm areas go gray or blue, it is too heavy.

Photos

Real people, doing real things

People are at the heart of how MassChallenge looks. Choose photos of real work and real people with some warmth in every frame, and give the words somewhere calm to sit.

Three treatments

  • Headline

    01

    Overlay

    Heroes, social posts and event graphics. A thin hero overlay, from Navy-black on the text side to Ocean toward the subject, at 15 to 30% opacity, so warm light still glows through.

  • 02

    Full color

    Content photos, spaces and people. No overlay and no duotone.

  • News

    03

    Accent rescue

    A cool photo with no warmth gets one warm accent, Yellow, Orange or Lime, on a tag, a rule or a subhead.

The previews are color diagrams, not photos. The hero overlay is a linear gradient from#051925 to Ocean #037AA6 at 15 to30% opacity, running toward the subject: the text side dark, the subject side clear. The teal duotone is retired.

What to show

  • Every image carries some warmth: skin tones, natural or lamp light, wood, brick, city lights.
  • Real work and real people: labs, workbenches, prototypes, conversations.
  • Mostly candid moments, with the occasional founder hero portrait.
  • Asymmetric frames that leave room for text, and a range that includes wide shots.
  • Judge by eye: if warm areas go gray or blue, the overlay is too heavy.

How to shoot and edit

  • Natural light and balanced exposure, with soft flash for dark rooms.
  • Shoot RAW, and edit only exposure and contrast.
  • No artistic filters and no black and white.

Avoid

  • People on phones or looking bored.
  • Visible alcohol.
  • Crowds from behind.
  • Cluttered compositions.
  • Black and white.
  • Generic stock: handshakes, posed smiles, abstract tech with no people.
  • Obviously AI generated images: waxy skin, warped hands or text, impossible lighting, glowing suns, lens flares, holograms.
  • Full-frame cool duotone, which is retired because it reads clinical.
  • Overlays so heavy the subject disappears.

Sources

  • Our own professional event photography comes first.
  • Licensed stock photography that MassChallenge holds a license for may be used when it looks believable and follows these rules. Ask MassChallenge Marketing for it.
  • Location photos for each site, by city in the US and by country elsewhere, follow the same rules.
  • Never present an AI generated image as a real founder, partner or event.

What works and what does not

  • Do

    People first

    People doing something together: talking, building, pitching, celebrating.

  • Do

    Room to breathe

    A calm area, such as a wall or the sky, where a headline or the logo can sit.

  • Do

    Words on a solid block

    When the photo is busy, add the hero overlay or set the text on a Navy, Black or Deep Teal block.

  • Do

    Crop on purpose

    Keep heads whole and leave people looking into the page.

  • Do

    White logo on a dark, calm part

    The full color logo goes only on White or Warm White.

  • Don't

    Empty rooms and clutter

    Empty offices and cluttered compositions say little about us. Show real work and real people.

  • Don't

    Text on a busy part

    Words over a busy patch of a photo are hard to read.

  • Don't

    Cut at the joints

    Never crop people at the neck, elbows or knees.

  • Don't

    Stretched or squashed

    Resize from a corner so the photo keeps its proportions.

  • Don't

    Filters and effects

    No artistic filters, black and white, heavy vignettes or color casts. Edit only exposure and contrast.

  • Don't

    Generic stock

    No handshakes, posed smiles, light bulbs or abstract tech with no people.

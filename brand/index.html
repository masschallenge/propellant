Graphic devices

The shapes, frames and treatments that make MassChallenge work recognizable. Build them from the palette and the logo files.

Geometric pattern

Bright teal and Pale teal triangles, with an Oswald label in capitals on top, such as ACCELERATOR or CHALLENGE. This is the brand pattern.

Use it for: Covers and headers for a program or a page.

Set the label in Ink (5.4:1 on Bright teal, 13.2:1 on Pale teal), or in White on a solid Deep teal panel (10.7:1). Never set it in White straight on the triangles: White on Bright teal is only 2.7:1, below the 3:1 that even large text needs.

Teal duotone

A photo washed in a gradient from Deep teal to Bright teal.

Use it for: Backgrounds behind text, and section headers.

Hexagon network frame

A circular photo crop inside a pale teal network of hexagons and lines.

Use it for: A single photo beside text, such as on a partner page.

Co-brand lockup

The MassChallenge logo, a plus sign and the partner logo, on white with a thin teal border, or a teal panel with the white MassChallenge logo beside the partner.

Use it for: Work made with a partner. Use the logo files, never a redrawn logo.

Contrast is shown with white and with Ink as a ratio, then graded: AA means any text, AA Large means text 18pt or larger and graphics only, Fail means neither. The web palette is the standard everywhere: web, Office, Google, print, the studio and the Claude skill.

Photos

Real people, doing real things

People are at the heart of how MassChallenge looks. Choose photos of our community at work, together, and give the words somewhere calm to sit.

What works and what does not

  • Do

    People first

    People doing something together: talking, building, pitching, celebrating.

  • Do

    Room to breathe

    A calm area, such as a wall or the sky, where a headline or the logo can sit.

  • Do

    Words on a Deep teal panel

    When the photo is busy, put the text on a Deep teal band or panel beside or over it.

  • Do

    Crop on purpose

    Keep heads whole and leave people looking into the page.

  • Do

    White logo on a dark, calm part

    The full color logo goes only on white.

  • Don't

    Empty rooms and objects

    Empty offices and objects on desks say little about us.

  • Don't

    Text on a busy part

    Words over a busy patch of a photo are hard to read.

  • Don't

    Cut at the joints

    Never crop people at the neck, elbows or knees.

  • Don't

    Stretched or squashed

    Resize from a corner so the photo keeps its proportions.

  • Don't

    Filters and effects

    No filters, heavy vignettes, color casts or phone app effects.

  • Don't

    Stock clichés

    No handshakes, light bulbs, puzzle pieces or chess pieces.

These are diagrams, not photos. The rules come from the photography and video guide.

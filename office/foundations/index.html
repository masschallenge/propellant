Designing documents, decks and spreadsheets

Everything MassChallenge sends out, from a one page memo to a pitch deck to a budget, should look like it came from the same team that built our website. This page sets the shared rules. The guides for each app show you where to find the settings, and the templates have everything set up already.

The rules come straight from Propellant, so a document and the website use the same colors, fonts and voice.

Start from a template

Section titled “Start from a template”

The fastest way to get every rule right is to start from a Propellant template. They have the colors, fonts, styles, logo and page setup built in.

App Template Guide
Word propellant-document.dotx Word
PowerPoint propellant-presentation.potx PowerPoint
Excel propellant-workbook.xltx Excel
Google Docs propellant-document.docx Google Docs
Google Slides propellant-presentation.pptx Google Slides
Google Sheets propellant-workbook.xlsx Google Sheets

Installing the templates explains how to add them to each app, and how an administrator can share them with everyone.

Fonts

Section titled “Fonts”
Font Use it for
Lato Everything: body text, headings, tables, labels and captions.
Oswald Only the big headline: a document title, a slide title or a sheet title. Always in capitals.

Oswald is a narrow, condensed font. It is made for short, large headlines and is hard to read in long text, so never use it for paragraphs, bullets or table text.

Getting the fonts

Section titled “Getting the fonts”

Both fonts are free, open source fonts from Google Fonts.

  • Google Docs, Sheets and Slides can use both. Open the font menu, choose More fonts and search for Lato and Oswald.
  • Microsoft Word, PowerPoint and Excel do not include them. Lato and Oswald are not among Microsoft 365’s cloud fonts, so each computer needs them installed. Ask IT to install them for you, or download them from Lato and Oswald and install them yourself. A Microsoft 365 administrator can also publish them as organization fonts so they work in Office on the web.

If a computer does not have the fonts, Office swaps in another font and the layout can shift.

Sending files outside MassChallenge

Section titled “Sending files outside MassChallenge”

People outside MassChallenge may not have Lato and Oswald. Before you send a Word or PowerPoint file that someone else will open:

  • Send a PDF when they only need to read it. A PDF keeps the fonts exactly.
  • Embed the fonts when they need to edit it. In Word or PowerPoint on Windows, choose File, Options, Save, then Embed fonts in the file, and leave “Embed only the characters used” off. Recent Word and PowerPoint for Mac support this too. Microsoft explains embedding. Embedded fonts do not show in Office on the web, and Excel cannot embed fonts.

Color

Section titled “Color”

Every template uses the Propellant theme. A theme is the set of twelve named colors and two fonts that Office and Google offer at the top of every color and font menu. Pick colors from the theme row, never from “More colors”, and your file stays on brand even if the theme is updated later.

Theme slot Name Hex Use it for
Dark 1 (text) Ink #1E282B Body text and headings
Light 1 (background) White #FFFFFF Page and slide backgrounds
Dark 2 Deep teal #044451 Title slides, section slides, table headers, cover pages
Light 2 Mist #F3F6F7 Alternate rows and quiet panels
Accent 1 Teal #065E6F Links and the first chart series
Accent 2 Yellow #FFC801 Highlights, always with Ink text, and the second chart series
Accent 3 Slate #4B575B Secondary text and the third chart series
Accent 4 Bright teal #0FACBD Accents on Deep teal and the fourth chart series
Accent 5 Deep teal #044451 The fifth chart series
Accent 6 Gray #848F92 Comparison data, such as last year
Hyperlink Teal #065E6F Links
Followed hyperlink Deep teal #044451 Visited links

Two more brand colors sit outside the theme on purpose:

  • Pale teal #D9F6FA tints a callout box or a highlighted row.
  • Mid teal #068DA4 is a supporting accent for shapes and icons.
  • Magenta #A30253 means something is wrong or late. It is not in the theme so it never ends up in a chart or a decoration by accident.

Using color

Section titled “Using color”

  • Most of the page is White, with Ink text. Color is an accent, not a background for everything.

  • Use Deep teal for big brand moments: a title slide, a section divider, a cover. On Deep teal use White text and Bright teal accents, never Teal text.

  • Keep headings in Ink. Teal is for links and things people can click.

  • Never show meaning with color alone. Pair every status color with a word, such as On track or Late.

  • Keep text readable: a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 with its background, as the WCAG guidelines require. These pairings pass:

    Text Background Contrast
    Ink White, Mist or Pale teal 13.3:1 or more
    Ink Yellow 9.7:1
    Slate White or Mist 6.9:1 or more
    Teal White 7.4:1
    White Deep teal 10.8:1
    White Teal 7.4:1
    Bright teal Deep teal 3.9:1, so only for text 18 pt or larger

    Bright teal, Mid teal, Yellow and Gray are too light for text on White.

Charts

Section titled “Charts”
  • A chart with one series uses Teal. Most charts need only one.
  • Charts take their colors from the theme in the order above, so do not recolor series by hand.
  • With two or more series, show the values or series names on the chart itself, not only in a legend. Yellow, Bright teal and Gray are light against White, so labels are what make them readable, and they help anyone who cannot tell the colors apart.
  • Keep to four series or fewer. Fold the rest into Other, or split the chart.
  • Use one value axis. Two measures on different scales belong in two charts.
  • Never use Magenta for a series: it means something is wrong.

Type styles

Section titled “Type styles”

Use the built-in styles in the templates instead of formatting text by hand. Styles keep a long document consistent, build the table of contents and tell screen readers how the document is organized.

Case

Section titled “Case”

Only Oswald titles are in capitals. Everything else, headings and table headers included, is in sentence case: a capital first letter and lower case after it, except for names.

The logo

Section titled “The logo”

Use the logo files in assets/logos, never a copy from a website or an old file.

Background Logo
White or a light color Full color: masschallenge-logo-horizontal-color.png
Deep teal, a dark color or a dark photo White: masschallenge-logo-horizontal-white.png
One color printing Black: masschallenge-logo-horizontal-black.png
  • Put the logo once per page or slide, in a corner. The templates place it for you.
  • Leave clear space around it equal to half its height.
  • Never stretch, recolor, outline or redraw it, and never put the full color logo on Deep teal.
  • In a document, keep the horizontal logo at least a third of an inch (8 mm) tall so the wordmark stays readable. Use the MC monogram alone only where MassChallenge is already named.

The brand guidelines have the full logo rules.

Layout

Section titled “Layout”
  • Leave space. White space makes a page easier to read. Do not fill every corner.
  • Align to the margins. Line up text, images and charts on the same left edge.
  • One idea per slide, one table per sheet, one purpose per document.
  • Round the corners of photos and panels slightly, about 0.12 inch (3 mm), as the website does with 12 pixels. Logos and charts stay square.
  • Use real lists. Use the bullet and numbering buttons, never typed dashes or asterisks.

Writing

Section titled “Writing”

Documents sound like the rest of MassChallenge: bold and human, direct and plain spoken.

  • Put the point first: the decision, the ask or the takeaway.
  • Use short sentences and everyday words. Write “we” for MassChallenge and “you” for the reader.
  • Make a slide title say the takeaway, not the topic: “Applications grew in every program”, not “Applications”.
  • Skip buzzwords such as leverage, seamless and robust, and skip exclamation marks.
  • Use commas, colons and periods. Do not use em dashes or en dashes.

Accessibility

Section titled “Accessibility”

Everything we send should work for people who use screen readers, magnifiers or high contrast settings.

  • Use the built-in title, heading and list styles so the structure is real, not just visual.
  • Give every photo, chart and diagram alt text that says what it shows. Mark purely decorative images as decorative.
  • Give every slide a unique title, even if you hide it.
  • Run the checker before you share: in Word, PowerPoint and Excel choose Review, then Check Accessibility. Google Docs, Sheets and Slides have no built-in checker, so check headings, alt text and slide titles by hand.
  • Use link text that says where it goes, such as “the application guide”, never “click here”.

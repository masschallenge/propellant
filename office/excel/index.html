Excel

This guide covers budgets, trackers, program data and reports in Microsoft Excel. Read the foundations first: they set the colors, fonts and voice this guide applies.

Start from the template

Section titled “Start from the template”
  1. Install propellant-workbook.xltx as described in installing the templates.
  2. In Excel, choose File, then New, then Personal (or Shared), and pick Propellant workbook.
  3. The template has two sheets: Start here, with the logo, a title and how to use the template, and Example data, with a styled table and a chart. Replace the example rows with your own data.

To bring an existing workbook onto the brand, choose Page Layout, then Themes, then Browse for Themes, and pick Propellant.thmx. Charts and cell colors chosen from the theme switch over at once.

Sheets

Section titled “Sheets”
  • Start every workbook with a Start here sheet: what the workbook answers, who it is for, where the data comes from, and when it was last updated.
  • Name every sheet for what it holds, such as Budget 2026 or Applications, never Sheet1.
  • Keep one table per sheet, starting near the top left, with no blank rows or columns inside it.
  • Turn off gridlines on summary and presentation sheets with View, then uncheck Gridlines. Keep them on sheets where people enter data.

Titles and headers

Section titled “Titles and headers”
Element Style
Sheet title Oswald, 24 pt, Deep teal, typed in capitals, in the first rows
Description under the title Lato, 11 or 12 pt, Slate
Table header row Lato Bold, 11 pt, White on Deep teal
Cells Lato, 11 pt, Ink
Alternate rows Mist fill, or none

Header text is in sentence case and includes the unit, such as Budget (USD) or Duration (days).

Numbers

Section titled “Numbers”
  • Right align numbers and left align text.
  • Use the same number of decimals down a column. Use a thousands separator: 12,400, not 12400.
  • Show negative numbers with a minus sign or in brackets, never in red alone.
  • Use real dates, not text that looks like a date, so they sort and filter.
  • Freeze the header row with View, then Freeze Panes, then Freeze Top Row.
  • Write formulas so others can follow them: name important cells or ranges, and avoid typing a number into a formula when it belongs in its own labeled cell.

Tables and filters

Section titled “Tables and filters”

The header row and Mist stripes in the template are applied directly, so they look the same in Excel and in Google Sheets. To add filters and sorting, select the data and choose Insert, then Table. When you do, pick a table style from the light row so it does not fight the header colors, or keep the template formatting and turn off banded rows.

Status and conditional formatting

Section titled “Status and conditional formatting”
  • Show status with a word in its own column, such as On track, At risk or Late. Color can support the word, but never replace it.
  • For conditional formatting, use the Pale teal #D9F6FA fill for highlights and Yellow #FFC801 with Ink text for items that need attention. Keep Magenta #A30253 for things that are wrong.
  • Avoid data bars and icon sets in shared workbooks: they are hard to read and do not survive every export.

Charts

Section titled “Charts”
  • Insert charts with Insert, then Recommended Charts. They take their colors from the theme.
  • Follow the chart rules in the foundations: one series in Teal, labels on the chart when there are more, four series at most, one axis.
  • Give the chart a title that states the takeaway, and label the axes with units.
  • Prefer bar and line charts. Avoid pie charts with more than three slices, 3D charts and secondary axes.
  • Add alt text: right click the chart and choose View Alt Text.

Printing and sharing

Section titled “Printing and sharing”
  • Set printing up front with Page Layout: landscape for wide tables, Fit All Columns on One Page, and the header row repeated with Print Titles.
  • Check accessibility with Review, then Check Accessibility.
  • Excel cannot embed fonts. People without Lato see a different font, so share a PDF when the look matters.

Things to avoid

Section titled “Things to avoid”
  • Merged cells inside tables. They break sorting, filtering and screen readers.
  • Colors picked from More Colors instead of the theme row.
  • Hidden rows or sheets with data others depend on.
  • Oswald anywhere but the sheet title.
